### The score

`seed_render.c`'s `best()` renders candidates 0 .. QUAL − 1 to the end of PLACE and keeps the highest score. A tie
goes to the lower candidate. The score reads only trig positions (steps before the length) and the RHYTHM, PLACE and
TAKE settings. Each term decides only when the ones before it are equal:

| Bits | Term | Value |
|---|---|---|
| 30..24 | count kept | RANDOM: 64 − \|trigs − `seed_share(DENS, grid positions)`\| (at least 0); other sources 64 |
| 23..17 | spread: largest gap | 64 − the largest gap between consecutive trigs, wrapping at the length; no trigs: 0 |
| 16..10 | spread: adjacent trigs | 64 − the gaps of at most one grid step (GRID for RANDOM, else 1); one trig has no neighbour |
| 9..0 | fit to the meter | RANDOM with BIAS ≠ 0: the trigs' mean `seed_rank(METR, BEAT, ROLE)` (reversed for BIAS < 0), >> 6; else 0 |

The spread terms do not change when the take is rotated, so at BIAS 0 the score does not pull trigs towards strong or
weak steps (`test_rotation`). Cost: one pass over the track, plus one `seed_rank` per trig when BIAS ≠ 0.

## Decisions taken

The lead's answers to the design note (2026-11-15):

1. **The score's terms, in order:** (a) count kept, as proposed; (b) spread: the smallest largest gap between
   consecutive trigs (wrapping at the length), then the fewest adjacent trigs; (c) fit to the meter only when BIAS ≠ 0,
   as the mean rank in BIAS's direction, as a final tie-break. At BIAS 0 the score must not pull trigs towards strong
   or weak steps (Alan's choice).
2. **Request 1 accepted:** the candidate is mixed only into RHYTHM and PLACE slots (`seed_rng.c`); the later layers
   may run once on the winner (`seed_render.c`). Condition: candidate 0's numbers stay exactly as before, so every
   existing golden vector is unchanged. Met: the 73 vectors there before F11 are all the same.
3. **Request 2 accepted:** QUAL > 1 may break I4 (raising DENS can change the winner). Documented in
   `docs/v1/score.md`; QUAL defaults to 1; I4's tests stay at QUAL 1.

This task's own choices:

4. **"Adjacent" is a gap of at most one grid step.** With GRID 2 no two trigs are ever one step apart, so a gap of
   1 would make the term useless there. SYNC can bring two trigs closer than GRID; those count too.
5. **BIAS counts only with RANDOM.** It is a RANDOM knob, hidden for the other sources, so a value left from earlier
   RANDOM use does not steer QUAL on EUCLID, MAP or COPY. The count term is RANDOM's only too: MAP's count has no
   asked value, and the other sources draw no count.
6. **Count kept and SKIP.** With SKIP, the count term prefers candidates with no trigs in the skipped steps, the start
   of the track. That is a preference for position, not rank, and it comes from (a) as agreed; it shows only with
   SKIP or MASK on.
7. **Candidates are scored after PLACE**, not after the whole chain. No layer after PLACE creates, moves or removes a
   trig (FLD stops at LANE2; ALL is ROTATE ALL's), so the result is the same. `test_dress` checks that the winner's
   fields equal those of the whole chain run on the winner's trigs.


`test_score` checks each term's value on hand-made tracks and their order; rotation invariance at BIAS 0 (300 random
tracks, every meter and role); ties (SOURCE = TRACK, EUCLID, RANDOM with BIAS ±100: QUAL 2..8 gives QUAL 1's take,
byte for byte) and I2; the dressing (400 random recipes: the winner equals the whole chain on its trigs, and dressing
knobs do not move a trig, I3 for QUAL); I6 at QUAL > 1; raising QUAL never lowers the take's score. It prints, over
1,000 takes on 16 steps:

| DENS, BIAS | | QUAL 1 | 2 | 4 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25, 0 | largest gap | 7.75 | 6.74 | 6.11 | 5.63 |
| | adjacent trigs | 0.84 | 0.55 | 0.32 | 0.18 |
| | on the beats (of 4,000 trigs) | 1,015 | 982 | 986 | 970 |
| 50, 0 | largest gap | 4.42 | 3.86 | 3.48 | 3.20 |
| | adjacent trigs | 3.74 | 3.22 | 2.84 | 2.44 |
| | on the beats (of 8,000) | 2,009 | 2,031 | 2,042 | 2,034 |
| 25, 60 | on the beats (of 4,000) | 2,869 | 3,113 | 3,392 | 3,721 |

At BIAS 0 a quarter of the trigs stay on the beats (checked within 15 %: a well-spread take of 4 in 16 has all or
none of its trigs on the beats, so the count varies widely).

**VARY at QUAL 2, 4 and 8** (`test_vary`; 500 consecutive takes, 16 steps, DENS 50): the share of the RANDOM
source's keys the winner of take n + 1 shares with the winner of take n, and how often the same candidate wins. The
test also checks that the render's trigs are those of the winner it computes.

| VARY | kept, QUAL 1 | 2 | 4 | 8 | same winner, QUAL 2 | 4 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 % | 0 | 0 | 0 | 52 % | 24 | 12 |
| 25 | 25 | 12 | 6 | 3 | 47 | 26 | 11 |
| 50 | 49 | 26 | 17 | 10 | 54 | 33 | 20 |
| 75 | 74 | 47 | 32 | 22 | 62 | 42 | 29 |
| 90 | 89 | 71 | 60 | 51 | 79 | 66 | 56 |
| 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 |

The share is close to P(same winner) × VARY, as the design note expected: a different winner shares no keys. The
dressing keeps VARY's share exactly, whichever candidate wins (answer 2).

## Open points

- **VARY with QUAL > 1** relates the takes shown much less than VARY says (table above): at VARY 50 and QUAL 4,
  17 % of the keys are kept. This is the agreed design, and `docs/v1/score.md` says so. If it is too weak in use, a
  later change could prefer take n − 1's winning candidate when the scores are close, at the cost of rendering take
  n − 1 too.
- The terms' order and decision 4 are cheap to change before 0.9's vectors freeze.
