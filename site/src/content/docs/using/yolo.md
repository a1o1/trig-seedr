**YOLO** rolls a whole recipe for the track you are on, so you can hear ideas without setting every page yourself. It is a tool on the **TOOLS** page.

![TOOLS with YOLO: a roll pending, the dot in the title bar](../../../assets/screens/yolo.png)

| Knob | Name | Range | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | TOOL | ROT ALL, YOLO | YOLO |
| B | WILD | 0–100 % | how far a roll goes (below) |
| C | ROLL | 0–9999 | which roll: the same ROLL, WILD and track give the same recipe again |

## Using it

1. Turn **RECORD** on, go to **TOOLS** and set **TOOL** to **YOLO**.
2. **YES** rolls: the recipe on every page is replaced by a new one and the take is written so you hear it. The strip shows the roll's number.
3. **YES** again: the next roll. Turn **ROLL** to go back to one you liked.
4. **FUNC + YES** keeps the roll: the take and the recipe stay, and you can go through the pages to see what it picked and change it.
5. **NO** puts everything back as it was before your first roll: the track's trigs **and** every page's settings. Leaving the track or pattern, closing Trig Seedr or turning RECORD off with a roll not kept does the same (NOT APPLIED).

Only the track you are on changes. YOLO never touches other tracks or the settings the pattern's tracks share (MAP's X, Y and CHAOS), and it keeps your meter (METR, BEAT) and the parameters your lock lanes are on.

## What a roll does

A setting picked at random over its whole range is mostly unusable: no trigs at all, a condition on every trig, a retrig on every step. So YOLO picks from ranges that sound like a drum part, and what the track plays matters: BD rolls kick-like rhythms, SD, RS and CP backbeats, CH, OH and CY busier hat lines, the toms and CB percussion.

A roll picks a **rhythm** (often the track's own trigs; otherwise RAND with the track's role, EUCLID with a pulse count that suits it, or MAP on the track's part) and then a few **layers** from: accents and a velocity shape, a few trig conditions or retrigs, a groove or a human feel, SYNC, a small tune, sample locks, a phrase over the pages.

| WILD | A roll |
|---|---|
| 0–39 | one or two gentle layers, mostly on the track's own trigs |
| 40–79 | two or three layers, other rhythms more often; notes from 50, phrases on long tracks |
| 80–100 | three or four layers, wider ranges, sample locks (from 70) |
